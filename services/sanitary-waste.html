Sanitary Waste Collection

Discreet, scheduled sanitary and hygiene waste collection for workplace washrooms — compared across licensed partners.

Get a Free Quote Upload Your Invoice

Sanitary waste covers feminine hygiene products, nappies, incontinence pads and similar washroom waste. It should not go in recycling, and in many workplaces it is collected separately from general waste for hygiene and to meet washroom regulations. Stratos Environmental compares licensed hygiene-waste partners for offices, gyms, schools, nurseries, pubs and public-facing premises.

What is included

  • Feminine hygiene products from washroom units
  • Nappies and incontinence products in staff or customer toilets
  • Related hygiene waste from baby-change and accessible toilets

This stream is often grouped with offensive waste. The right classification depends on whether the waste is infectious. Everyday sanitary waste from healthy occupants is typically treated as offensive/hygiene waste, not clinical.

How the service works

Wall-mounted or floor sanitary units are supplied and emptied on a regular cycle. Frequency depends on how busy the washrooms are — a small office may need monthly servicing; a gym, school or busy pub often needs more. Units are swapped or emptied hygienically so staff never handle the waste directly.

Who needs it

Any workplace with female staff or public toilets, plus nurseries, schools, gyms, hotels and hospitality. Combining sanitary units with general waste and recycling collections keeps washrooms compliant without a separate admin headache.

Frequently asked questions

Is sanitary waste the same as clinical waste?

Not usually. Everyday sanitary products from healthy people are typically offensive or hygiene waste. Clinical classification applies if the waste is infectious or from healthcare treatment.

How often should sanitary units be emptied?

It depends on footfall. Small offices may be monthly; busy gyms, schools and pubs often need more frequent servicing. We'll match the cycle to your premises.

Do I need this if I have a general waste bin?

Washroom regulations and hygiene best practice usually require dedicated sanitary units rather than asking staff to use the general bin. A proper unit is also more discreet.
