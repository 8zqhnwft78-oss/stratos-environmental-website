Sharps Waste Collection

Licensed, compliant collection of needles, scalpels and other sharps — compared across specialist carriers so healthcare and tattoo businesses stay safe and legal.

Sharps waste is any item that can cut or puncture skin: used needles, syringes, scalpels, stitch cutters, razors and similar instruments. It is a specialist hazardous stream, not general waste, and must be stored in approved sharps boxes and collected by a registered carrier. Stratos Environmental compares licensed clinical-waste partners so dental practices, clinics, tattoo studios, care homes and pharmacies get a compliant service without overpaying.

What counts as sharps waste

  • Used hypodermic needles and syringes
  • Scalpels, blades and stitch cutters
  • Broken glass from medical or tattoo use
  • Lancets and similar puncture items

Sharps contaminated with medicines or body fluids usually need a specific colour-coded container (commonly yellow or orange, depending on the waste). Mixing sharps into a general or recycling bin is unsafe and can be an offence.

How collection works

Approved leak-proof sharps boxes are delivered to your premises. You fill them to the fill line only — never overfill or recap needles — and they are exchanged on a schedule that matches how quickly you generate waste. Completed boxes are taken to a licensed treatment facility, typically for high-temperature incineration, with consignment notes kept for your records.

Who needs this service

Dental and medical practices, hospitals, pharmacies that offer vaccinations, tattoo and piercing studios, veterinary surgeries and some care settings. If you produce even a small volume of sharps, you still need a proper contract — volume is not an excuse to use the domestic bin.

Related services

Sharps often sit alongside clinical waste, offensive waste and dental or tattoo collections. We can arrange these together.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put needles in my general waste bin?

No. Sharps must go in approved sharps containers and be collected by a registered specialist carrier. Putting needles in general waste is unsafe and can breach your Duty of Care.

Do tattoo studios need sharps collection?

Yes. Needles and similar items from tattooing and piercing are sharps waste and must be collected under a compliant clinical/sharps contract.

How often are sharps boxes collected?

It depends on how quickly you fill them. Many small practices exchange boxes monthly or quarterly; busier sites need more frequent collections. We'll match the schedule to your usage.
