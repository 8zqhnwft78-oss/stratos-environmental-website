Offensive Waste Collection

Compliant collection of non-infectious offensive waste — PPE, nappies, hygiene products — compared across licensed carriers.

Offensive waste is non-infectious waste that is unpleasant to handle: used PPE, nappies, incontinence products, sanitary items, and some healthcare hygiene waste (for example cardboard vomit bowls) where there is no known infection risk. It is not the same as infectious clinical waste, but it still needs the right bags, bins and a registered collector. Stratos Environmental Ltd compares licensed partners for care homes, clinics, nurseries and similar sites.

Typical offensive waste

  • Used gloves, masks and aprons that are not infectious
  • Nappies and incontinence waste from non-infectious sources
  • Sanitary products (often serviced as a washroom unit)
  • Some non-infectious healthcare hygiene items

If waste is known or suspected to be infectious, it belongs on a clinical waste contract instead. Getting the colour-coding wrong is a common compliance failure in care and healthcare settings.

Storage and collection

Offensive waste is usually stored in tiger-stripe (yellow and black) bags in a dedicated bin, collected on a schedule that prevents overflow and odour. Frequency is higher in care homes and nurseries than in a small office with only occasional hygiene waste.

Related streams

Many sites also need sanitary, sharps and general collections. We arrange them as one comparison so you are not managing three suppliers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between offensive and clinical waste?

Offensive waste is non-infectious but unpleasant to handle. Clinical waste is infectious or from treatment and needs a stricter disposal route. If in doubt, we will help you classify it correctly with a licensed carrier.

What bags should we use?

Offensive waste is commonly stored in yellow-and-black tiger-stripe bags. Your carrier will confirm the exact colour coding for your site.

Do offices need offensive waste collection?

Most small offices only need sanitary units for washrooms. Care, nursery, clinic and some hospitality sites are more likely to need a full offensive-waste bin.
