Commercial Waste Prices

What business waste collection really costs in 2026 — indicative prices by bin size and waste stream, what drives the cost, and how to pay less.

“How much should I be paying for commercial waste collection?” is the question we’re asked most — and the honest answer is that prices vary enormously. There is no fixed national rate; what you pay depends on your bin size, how often you’re collected, the type of waste, your location and the deal you signed. This guide gives you realistic, indicative figures and, more importantly, shows you how to make sure you’re not overpaying.

What drives the price

  • Collection frequency — the biggest single factor. Each extra weekly collection adds cost, so being over-serviced is the most common reason businesses overpay.
  • Bin size and volume — you pay for capacity, so a half-empty bin is wasted money.
  • Waste type — general waste costs more to dispose of than recycling; food and clinical waste carry their own handling costs.
  • Location — dense city-centre sites, restricted access and timed collections (common in London) push prices up.
  • Contract length and terms — rolling contracts with annual uplifts inflate bills over time.
  • Standing charges and VAT — watch for bin rental, transfer-note fees and other add-ons; commercial waste is subject to VAT.

Prices by waste stream

As a general rule, recycling collections are cheaper than general waste, because recyclable materials have value and avoid landfill tax. Dry mixed recycling lets you combine plastics, metals, paper and card in one bin. Food waste is priced separately and is worth pulling out of general waste because it’s heavy. Glass is usually a dedicated collection, important for pubs and restaurants. Clinical waste is specialist and priced accordingly.

Prices by business type

  • Small office or shop — often £30–£60 a month for a single general bin plus recycling.
  • Café — more, due to added food waste and higher frequency.
  • Restaurant or pub — frequently £150–£400+ a month across general, food and glass streams.
  • Warehouse or larger site — varies widely with volume and container type.

Hidden costs to watch for

The headline per-lift rate isn’t the whole story. Look out for bin rental / standing charges, minimum contract terms, automatic annual price increases, side-waste and contamination charges, and container delivery fees. When we compare providers we look at the total cost of ownership, not just the sticker price.

How to pay less

The quickest win is almost always to compare the market — prices for identical services differ significantly between carriers. Beyond that: right-size your bins and frequency, shift volume into cheaper recycling, separate food waste, avoid contamination charges, and keep an eye on contract renewal dates. Frequently asked questions

How much does commercial waste collection cost?

Indicatively, a 240L general waste bin costs around £6–£12 per collection and a 1100L bin around £20–£38, excluding VAT and standing charges. A small business often spends £30–£60 a month; busy restaurants and pubs can pay £150–£400+ across multiple streams. Your actual price depends on frequency, volume, waste type and location.

Why do commercial waste prices vary so much?

Because pricing is driven by many factors — frequency, bin size, waste type, location, access and contract terms — and different carriers price the same service very differently. That's exactly why comparing the market saves money.

Is there VAT on commercial waste collection?

Yes, commercial waste collection services are subject to VAT, so remember to factor that into any quoted price.

How can I reduce my commercial waste bill?

Right-size your bins and frequency, recycle more (recycling is usually cheaper than general waste), separate food waste, avoid contamination charges, watch for auto-renewing contracts, and compare providers regularly.
