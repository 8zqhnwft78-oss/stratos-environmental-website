Simpler Recycling: What Businesses Must Do

England’s new workplace recycling rules affect almost every business. Here’s what you need to separate, and by when.

Get a Free Quote Upload Your Invoice

“Simpler Recycling” is the government’s programme to standardise recycling across England. For businesses, it changes how waste must be separated at the point of collection. If you run a workplace, these rules almost certainly apply to you.

What is Simpler Recycling?

The aim is to end the postcode lottery of different recycling rules by making the same core waste streams collected everywhere — from homes, businesses and public bodies. For businesses it means presenting recycling separately from general (residual) waste.

What must businesses separate?

  • Dry recyclables — plastic, metal, glass, paper and card (paper and card may need to be kept separate from other dry recyclables by some collectors)
  • Food waste — collected separately for recycling
  • Residual (general) waste — everything that can’t be recycled

Who is affected and when?

  • Workplaces with 10 or more employees were required to separate these streams from 31 March 2025.
  • Micro-firms (fewer than 10 employees) must comply by 31 March 2027.

“Employees” is counted across the whole business, not per site.

How to comply without overpaying

You don’t need a bin for every material if your collector offers dry mixed recycling (several dry materials in one bin) — check what your provider accepts. The key is having the right containers, clear signage, and a compliant collection contract in place.

Penalties

The Environment Agency can issue compliance notices to businesses that don’t separate their waste correctly. Beyond the legal risk, separating recycling usually reduces costs, because recycling collections are often cheaper than general waste.
