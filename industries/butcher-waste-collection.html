Butcher Waste Collection

Compliant food, packaging and general waste collection for butchers — compared across licensed partners who understand meat-counter waste.

Butchers generate heavy food waste, animal by-products that may need a specialist route, plus cardboard, plastic trays and general waste. Getting the classification right matters: some meat waste is Category 3 animal by-product and cannot simply go in a standard food caddy. Stratos Environmental compares licensed carriers and will flag when a specialist ABP collection is required rather than a generic food-waste bin.

Typical streams

  • Food waste and trimmings where the carrier accepts them
  • Animal by-product collections where legally required
  • Cardboard and plastic packaging recycling
  • General waste for residual rubbish

We will not pretend every butcher can use the same food-waste bin. If your waste is ABP, we match you with a carrier set up for it.

Hygiene and frequency

Meat waste goes off quickly. Collections are usually frequent, with bins stored cold or collected often enough to avoid odour and pests. High-street shops with little storage need smaller bins and more lifts rather than one large container.

Frequently asked questions

Is butcher waste the same as restaurant food waste?

Not always. Some butcher waste is animal by-product and needs a specialist collection. We will help you classify it rather than putting everything in a standard food bin.

How often do butchers need collection?

Typically several times a week because the waste is heavy and perishable. Exact frequency depends on throughput and storage.

Can packaging be recycled?

Clean cardboard usually can. Food-soiled trays and films often cannot. Separating clean cardboard is still worth doing.
