Commercial waste collection prices in London

There is no single “London price” for business waste. What you pay is driven by four things: the volume of waste you produce, the bin size you use, how often you’re collected, and the waste stream (general waste is dearer than recycling). On top of the per-collection (per-lift) cost you may see a bin rental or standing charge, and every commercial contract is subject to VAT.

Choosing the right bin size

Getting your bin size and frequency right is where most savings are found. Pay for a 1100L bin that’s half empty each week and you’re burning money; under-provision and you’ll get side-waste charges or overflowing bins. The common commercial options are:

  • 240L wheelie bin — small offices, salons and shops (about 3–4 bin bags).
  • 360L wheelie bin — slightly larger premises or higher footfall.
  • 660L wheelie bin — busy cafés, small restaurants and mid-size offices.
  • 1100L wheelie bin — restaurants, pubs, larger retail and offices (about 14–18 bags).
  • FEL / REL containers — front- and rear-end-loader bins for high-volume sites and estates.

Recycling and Simpler Recycling in London

Recycling is not just good practice — for most London businesses it is now a legal requirement, and it usually reduces your bill because recycling collections are frequently cheaper than general waste. Under England’s Simpler Recycling rules, workplaces with 10 or more employees have had to separate their recyclables and food waste from general waste since 31 March 2025; smaller micro-firms must comply by 31 March 2027.

In practice that means presenting three broad streams: dry recyclables (plastic, metal, glass, paper and card), food waste, and residual general waste. Many London providers offer dry mixed recycling so several materials can go in one bin, which is ideal where space for lots of separate containers simply doesn’t exist.

Food waste collection

Separate food waste collection is essential for London’s huge hospitality sector and any office with a canteen or kitchen. Food is heavy and, when mixed into general waste, drives up disposal costs and creates odour and pest problems — a real issue in dense, warm city-centre premises. Collected separately, food waste is typically recycled into energy through anaerobic digestion. Restaurants, pubs, cafés and hotels should treat food waste as a core, frequent stream.

Glass and other waste streams

Pubs, bars and restaurants across London get through enormous volumes of glass, which needs its own glass recycling collection for safety and recycling. Other common commercial streams we compare include mixed recycling, clinical and healthcare waste for dental and medical premises, and confidential waste for offices handling sensitive paperwork.

Your legal duties

Every London business has a legal Duty of Care for the waste it produces. In short, you must:

  • Store waste safely and separate recyclables in line with Simpler Recycling.
  • Only hand waste to a registered waste carrier — never an unlicensed “man with a van”.
  • Keep a waste transfer note for every collection, retained for at least two years.

Get this wrong and you risk fixed penalties or unlimited fines — and if your waste is fly-tipped by an unlicensed carrier, you can be held responsible. When you book through Stratos, every collection is arranged through a licensed carrier and the paperwork is handled for you. It’s also worth knowing the difference between business and household waste: you cannot legally put commercial waste in domestic council bins.

London-specific challenges

Waste in London comes with complications you won’t find in most of the country:

  • Timed collections and restricted hours. Many central boroughs, including the City of Westminster and the City of London, restrict when waste can be presented and collected on busy streets. Bins can’t always sit out on the pavement, so timed sacks or narrow collection windows are common.
  • Limited storage space. Older buildings and dense high streets often have nowhere to keep large bins, which pushes businesses toward smaller, more frequent collections or timed sack services.
  • ULEZ and congestion. The Ultra Low Emission Zone and traffic add cost and complexity for collection vehicles, which feeds into pricing.
  • Fly-tipping hot-spots. London has significant fly-tipping enforcement; using a properly licensed carrier protects you from liability.
  • Borough variation. Rules, enforcement and typical pricing genuinely differ between boroughs, so local knowledge matters.

What affects your waste price the most

If you want to control what you spend, it helps to know which levers actually move the price. In order of impact, they are: collection frequency (each additional weekly collection adds cost, so being collected more often than you need is the most common source of overspend); volume and bin size (paying for capacity you don’t fill is money wasted); waste type (general waste is dearer to dispose of than recycling, and food and clinical waste have their own handling costs); location and access (central boroughs, timed collections and awkward access all push prices up); and contract length and terms (rolling contracts with annual uplifts quietly inflate your bill over time). A good comparison optimises all five together rather than just chasing a low headline rate.

How to cut your London waste bill

  • Right-size your bins and frequency together — the biggest saving is usually a better-matched combination, not simply the cheapest bin.
  • Recycle more — shifting volume from general waste into cheaper recycling streams reduces cost and keeps you compliant.
  • Separate food waste — it’s heavy, and pulling it out of general waste lowers the weight of your most expensive stream.
  • Avoid contamination charges — clear signage and a little staff training stop recycling bins being rejected.
  • Watch for auto-renewals — know your end date and notice period so a contract doesn’t roll over with a price rise.
  • Compare the market — prices for identical services vary widely between carriers; comparing is the single biggest lever. Read our full cost-cutting guide.

Contracts, rolling renewals and switching

Most commercial waste contracts run for a fixed term and then roll over automatically, often with an annual price increase written into the small print. That’s why so many London businesses are quietly overpaying: they signed years ago and have never re-checked the market. Frequently asked questions

How much does commercial waste collection cost in London?

It depends on your bin size, how often you're collected, and the waste type. As an indicative guide, a 240L general waste bin might cost around £6–£12 per collection and a 1100L bin around £20–£38, excluding VAT and any standing charge. A small business often spends £30–£60 a month, while busy hospitality sites can run into several hundred. The best way to know is to get a free comparison quote.

Do I legally have to recycle in London?

Yes. Under England's Simpler Recycling rules, workplaces with 10 or more employees have had to separate dry recyclables and food waste from general waste since 31 March 2025, and businesses with fewer than 10 employees must comply by 31 March 2027.

Can I use my council's household bins for business waste?

No. Waste produced by a business is commercial waste and must be collected under a separate commercial contract with a registered carrier. Putting business waste in domestic council bins can be an offence.
